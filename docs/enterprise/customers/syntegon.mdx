# Global technology company Syntegon puts 500+ RAG agents to work with Open WebUI

![Syntegon](/images/enterprise/customers/syntegon/hero.png)

## Overview

:::info
Syntegon built Syntia (Syntegon Intelligence Assistance) on Open WebUI and hosts it on AWS. It gives employees across engineering, operations, compliance and other functions one SSO-protected, fully auditable platform for 8+ AI models and RAG agents.
:::

- **Industry**: Global technology company
- **Company size**: 7,300 employees at 49 locations in more than 20 countries; headquartered in Stuttgart, Germany

## About Syntegon

[Syntegon](https://www.syntegon.com/) is a leading global technology company and strategic life cycle partner to the pharmaceutical, biotech and food industries. Headquartered in Stuttgart, Germany, it employs 7,300 people at 49 locations in more than 20 countries. With over 160 years of industry expertise, Syntegon delivers solutions that help people live better, stay healthier and trust the products they rely on every day.

## The Challenge: Scaling AI responsibly

Syntegon needed to scale AI access across a global, multi-site engineering organization while keeping strict data governance, compliance and full control over AI models and data sources. Its leadership set a goal: give every employee enterprise-grade AI capabilities without sacrificing security and compliance.

Syntegon found that public SaaS AI tools posed unacceptable risks around data residency and IP exposure. The company also found that commercial SaaS tools offered:

- too little control over data residency,
- no way to integrate proprietary internal data sources securely,
- limited choice of models, and
- no enterprise-grade role-based access aligned to Syntegon’s identity management.

Given how strategically important AI is to the company’s future, vendor lock-in was also a major concern.

Syntegon’s non-negotiable requirements were:

- Deployment in Syntegon-controlled cloud infrastructure (AWS, EU region)
- SSO with Microsoft Entra ID, plus role-based access through IAM
- Support for multiple LLM providers (Azure OpenAI and AWS Bedrock) through a unified gateway (LiteLLM)
- A Work Council and data protection officer (DPO) approval pathway that complies with local regulation
- A structured AI literacy training program that every employee must complete, covering both the strategic benefits of AI and the responsible boundaries of its use

## The Solution: Syntia on AWS

Open WebUI’s open architecture, permissive licensing and enterprise-ready features let Syntegon rapidly deploy a fully self-hosted, enterprise-hardened AI platform without customizing the codebase. Those features include RAG, Model Context Protocol (MCP), SSO, multi-model support and an extensible plugin framework.

LiteLLM proved decisive. Integrated as a unified, cost-controlled gateway, it routes traffic across Azure OpenAI, AWS Bedrock and future AI models. Open WebUI’s configurability also lets Syntegon’s security teams enforce strict governance policies, including disabling code execution and blocking unauthorized webhooks.

- **Compute and orchestration**: Amazon EKS (managed Kubernetes) on AWS Fargate
- **Storage and database**: Amazon Aurora (PostgreSQL-compatible) with the pgvector extension for RAG embeddings; AWS EFS for persistent shared storage across pods; daily AWS RDS snapshots
- **Networking**: All application workloads run in private subnets of an AWS VPC, with no direct inbound internet access to pods
- **Secrets**: AWS Secrets Manager for all sensitive credentials, and AWS KMS for encryption at rest
- **CI/CD**: GitOps-based deployment, with Terraform for infrastructure as code and Kubernetes manifests managed in Git. Planned updates are tested in staging before production, with documented rollback plans.
- **Logging and monitoring**: An OpenTelemetry Collector for traces and metrics, Prometheus for metrics storage, Grafana dashboards for visualization and alerting, AWS SNS for email alerts, and the LiteLLM dashboard for per-model usage and cost
- **Security controls**: MFA via the identity provider, device compliance, role-based access by group, enforced data residency, moderation and guardrails, audit exports to SIEM, and egress restrictions

### Models and data handling

All model routing runs through a LiteLLM gateway deployed inside Syntegon’s Kubernetes cluster.

- **Azure OpenAI**: GPT-5 for high-quality tasks; GPT-5-mini as the default for cost-effective, high-volume tasks; GPT-4.1 for complex reasoning; GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.1; and GPT 1.5 for image generation
- **AWS Bedrock**: Claude Sonnet 4.5 and 4.6 for advanced reasoning and long context; Pixtral Large 25.02 for vision and text; and the open-source gpt-oss-120b

GPT-5-mini is every user’s default, and users can choose other models from the full catalogue. Cost-sensitive or high-volume tasks go to GPT-5-mini or gpt-oss-120b. High-sensitivity or complex reasoning tasks can use Claude Sonnet or GPT-5.

RAG uses Azure OpenAI text-embedding-3-large with pgvector on Aurora, which Syntegon prefers for reliability across multiple pods. Sources include:

- internal knowledge bases uploaded to the Syntia Workspace,
- SharePoint document libraries, through the Microsoft Graph API,
- Confluence pages, through the Atlassian integration.

Syntia handles PDFs, Office files, CSV, Markdown, HTML, JSON, images and audio. OCR and Azure Document Intelligence extract content from complex documents.

Sensitive data is protected in four ways:

- Azure AI Content Safety filters all Azure model inputs and outputs, and AWS Bedrock Guardrails apply equivalent filtering on AWS models.
- Admin chat access and admin exports are disabled to protect user privacy.
- Direct signup is disabled. Users sign in only through Entra ID SSO and are provisioned through the Syntegon Identity Manager.
- Daily backups are encrypted at rest.

## Adoption and Enablement

![Syntia model selector showing GPT, Claude, and other available models.](/images/enterprise/customers/syntegon/open-webui.png)

Syntia went live in production in December 2025, with access provisioned through an Azure Service Group. It has been updated iteratively since. Employees in eligible regions can onboard themselves through the Syntegon Identity Manager.

Syntegon supports adoption through:

- **Training and documentation**: dedicated Syntia training materials and e-learning modules, with Confluence and SharePoint as the single source of truth for Syntia documentation, including architecture, configuration, security and rollout guides.
- **AI Hub & Spoke sessions**: a central hub team owns the shared AI infrastructure, governance, approved tools and reusable components, while departments act as spokes that apply that foundation to their own problems.
- **Product Board meetings**: used to gather feedback, prioritize features and share updates. An AI Innovation Hub coordinates these meetings, use-case ideation and roadmap governance across departments.
- **Support and community**: office hours, functional team enablement sessions and a dedicated AI Hub community.

Syntia is used across engineering and R&D, operations and manufacturing, project management, sales, marketing and customer experience, finance and HR, and digital transformation. It supports German, English and other languages through Azure Speech multilingual neural models. LiteLLM virtual API keys let engineering teams build their own AI-powered applications on Syntia’s model infrastructure.
